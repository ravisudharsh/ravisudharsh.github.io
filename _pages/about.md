## About me
I am a computational researcher in the [Department of Fundamental Microbiology](https://www.unil.ch/dmf/en/home.html) at the [University of Lausanne (UNIL)](https://www.unil.ch/). Prior to my current position, I served as a Bioinformatics Lead at a precision sports medicine startup, before returning to academia. A Postdoctoral Fellowship in [Social Genomics](https://www.jacobscenter.uzh.ch/en/research/social_genomics.html) at the [Jacobs Center for Productive Youth Development, UZH Zürich](https://www.jacobscenter.uzh.ch/en.html) preceded my time in industry. I earned my doctoral degree from ETH Zürich in Chemical and Bioengineering with a focus on Systems Biology in the [CABSEL](https://engineering.buffalo.edu/chemical-biological/people/faculty-directory/core.host.html/content/shared/engineering/chemical-biological/profiles/faculty/gunawan-rudiyanto.detail.html) group. My pre-doctoral academic career includes a Masters degree at ETH Zürich and research stints at Harvard-MIT Health Sciences and Technology affiliated laboratories exploring mechanisms of chemotherapy tolerance in breast cancer cells.

My [research]({{ '/publications/' | relative_url }}) focuses on the investigation of metabolic networks and their application in understanding the biology of human aging and age-related diseases. Through the development of algorithms designed to predict metabolic perturbations incorporating differential transcriptomic data, I strive to illuminate the molecular sequence of events underlying aging and neurodegeneration. This systems-level reasoning, integrating multi-omics data, has driven my work across neurodegeneration, cancer, and metabolic disease.

During my [postdoctoral research at UZH](https://www.jacobscenter.uzh.ch/en/research/social_genomics.html), I investigated the impact of socio-economic and other exogenous gradients on transcriptional and methylation patterns in humans, and their implications for phenotypic outcomes. Subsequently, as a Bioinformatics Lead at the startup, I applied machine learning to multimodal data integrating molecular and biosensor measurements, developing pre-symptomatic prediction models for injury risk in professional athletes. **Currently**, as a Postdoctoral Fellow in the Pacheco Lab at UNIL, I extend these computational frameworks to characterize metabolic interactions and resource exchange dynamics in microbial communities, advancing generalizable approaches to metabolic modeling across biological systems.
